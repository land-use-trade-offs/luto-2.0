__Project Summary__

International biodiversity commitments under the Kunming\-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework \(GBF\) require nations to simultaneously pursue targets spanning ecosystem restoration \(Target 2\), area\-based conservation \(Target 3\), species recovery \(Target 4\), and climate\-resilient biodiversity management \(Target 8\)\. Yet these targets are rarely assessed together, and the land use trade\-offs and synergies that arise when they are stacked remain poorly understood\. Under a business\-as\-usual land use trajectory, achieving any single target may constrain or enable the others, with consequences for where restoration investment should be directed and which policy instruments are most effective\. Here we use the LUTO2 national land use optimisation model to assess the feasibility and land use outcomes of meeting GBF Targets 2, 3, 4 and 8 individually and in combination in Australia by 2030, and to determine how the choice of biodiversity prioritisation framework shapes those trade\-offs\.

__Aim__

Based on everything we've discussed, here are the aims:

The overarching aim of this study is to assess the feasibility of meeting Australia's Kunming\-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework \(GBF\) commitments by 2030 and quantify the trade\-offs and synergies that arise when biodiversity targets are pursued individually versus in combination\.

- To determine the land use changes, key actions, and spatial patterns required to meet each of GBF Targets 2, 3, 4, and 8 independently in Australia by 2030, and to evaluate how achievable each target is under existing development trajectories\.
- To identify where pursuing multiple GBF targets simultaneously reinforces or undermines progress on individual targets, and to determine which target combinations are most and least compatible under BAU land use constraints\.
- To determine which land use transitions, management interventions, and geographic regions contribute most to achieving biodiversity outcomes under individual and combined target scenarios, providing a basis for prioritising restoration investment\.
- To evaluate how the choice between the Australian Government's Species of National Environmental Significance \(SNES\) dataset and spatially modelled biodiversity priority layers \(Zonation/HCAS\) affects the land use patterns, feasibility assessment, and spatial distribution of actions required to meet GBF targets\.
- To generate national\-scale feasibility maps and actionable insights that can inform Australia's National Biodiversity Strategy and Action Plan, restoration investment decisions, and land sector policy design ahead of the 2030 GBF deadline\.
